## Context

SAÉ 3.01, a second-year BUT course project titled "Developing and deploying
an application", running from October 12, 2026 to January 22, 2027. Starting
from a need described in a few lines, each team must gather and formalize the
requirements, then build a secure client-server application backed by a
database, with attention to usability, software quality and security. Fifteen
teams of six or seven students work on it, each mentored by two teachers: ten
weeks of two half-days, then three full-time weeks.

The shared brief: help future students discover university life in Grenoble
through personalized services. The Université Grenoble Alpes welcomes nearly
60,000 students across some fifteen sites. After a creativity session, we
presented our pitches to the mentors on October 23; the one selected targets
new students facing financial hardship. Support exists, but it is scattered across
the university, the CROUS student services, the family allowance office, the
metropolitan area and local associations: many students find out about it too
late, or never.

## Goals

- Bring together in one place the support a new student needs: health, food,
  sport, financial aid.
- Offer a personal space filtered by the student's needs and sorted by
  distance, on a map.
- Reach any service in three clicks at most, on a phone first.
- Provide reliable information: every entry cites its source and the date it
  was last checked.
- Comply with GDPR and security practice: minimal data, account deletion,
  hashed passwords, HTTPS and an OWASP risk analysis.

## Work

### Phase 1: framing and analysis

In progress, due November 20, 2026. The project charter sets out the problem,
the scope prioritized with the MoSCoW method, a register of eight risks and the
schedule for all three phases.

The application will be a responsive web app, designed for phones, with no
native app. When signing up, students tick their interests and needs by theme,
never as medical data; their address is optional, as the campus is enough. The
first sections cover health, with the university's psychologists and doctors
and a map of health centers, and food, with a feed of free food distributions.
An admin area will let staff enter services and events, each with its source.
Maps will rely on Leaflet and OpenStreetMap, geocoding on the IGN's
Géoplateforme.

This phase also includes a survey and interviews with students, personas,
requirements, user stories, use cases, the class diagram, low-fidelity mockups
and a feasibility study.

### Style guide

The style guide follows the Université Grenoble Alpes' current identity, taken
from its website rather than from the 2016 guidelines: orange and night blue,
with the Calistoga and Open Sans typefaces. Designed for phones, it targets
WCAG 2.2 level AA, checked against the RGAA, the French accessibility
framework: a contrast matrix sets which colors each kind of text may use, and
nothing overflows at a width of 320 px.

### Organization

The team works in sprints tracked on Jira, with Git for code and deliverables.
Each session opens with a ten-minute stand-up, and a task is only done once a
peer has reviewed it and its tests pass. Roles were assigned based on the
results of the Belbin test.
